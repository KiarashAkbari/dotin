========================================================================
feature frame performance, and the external signal, on a scratch cluster at the brief's scale
========================================================================
what produced this file, in one command (from ``stage-4/``, on the interpreter with the dev
extra)::

    python3 docs/evidence/frame-performance-sandbox.py \
      | tee docs/evidence/frame-performance-sandbox-run.txt

read these numbers as what they are:

* the corpus is SYNTHETIC and the machine is the agent sandbox («سندباکس»). No number below is a
  measurement of the project's own database, and none of them closes checklist 5.3 -- the row values
  are invented by ``frame-performance-synthetic.py``, at 100,000 orders, against the shipped schema,
  views, migrations and queries. The sandbox has no route to the student's PostgreSQL; that run is the
  one that decides the ceiling, and the command for it is ``docs/evidence/frame-performance-run.py``;
* what IS established here is the plan and the shape: the frame comes out at 32 columns / 29 feature
  columns against ``MINIMUM_FEATURES = 10``, the timed statement lands under the 500 ms ceiling on a
  production-sized corpus, and the two live guard modules run for real instead of skipping;
* the five timed runs inside this capture sit between 357.7 and 371.3 ms, mean 366.5 ms: under the
  500 ms ceiling, but by a margin that would not survive a smaller box, and this file records one
  procedure, not a distribution. The plan says where the margin goes: a ``Hash`` node with
  ``Hash Batches: 2`` and 2232 temp blocks read / 2938 written, which is ``work_mem`` spilling, and
  the first thing to look at if a real run comes out over the line;
* the log line ``tree: ... 3 tracked file(s) differ`` is the truth of the capture: the harness, its
  seeder and the tests for both were uncommitted while the measurement ran, and the commit that carries
  this file is the one after it. A performance log is a record of a moment, not of a release;
* unittest's elapsed-time clause is scrubbed (``in <t>s``) so two runs can be compared byte for byte.
  The measured durations are not scrubbed and will not repeat: five of them are printed on purpose.
