column-audit-run.txt -- the audit table's evidence, column by column.

Every number below is the output of a witness emitted by
scripts/audit_columns.py and executed against the sandbox Olist database
(docs/evidence/phase1-full-load.txt records how it was built and reconciled to
the phase-0 baseline). The verdicts and their reasons are the student's
artefact: the numbers do not decide them, they constrain them. The CSV at
docs/evidence/column_audit.csv is the machine-readable version of the same
table and is what capstone.audit.require_allowed reads at feature-build time.

Two measured facts change how the witnesses must be read, and both are about
the audit's own method rather than about any column:

1. `at_or_before_approval` is the right question for a column that records an
   event, and the wrong question for a column that records a promise.
   order_estimated_delivery_date is filled 99,441 times and is dated about 23
   days after approval by construction; shipping_limit_date is filled 112,650
   times and sits 6.2 days after approval on average. Neither is a leak. The
   test that separates them from a real one is whether orders that never
   advanced carry the value at all: 630 of 630 created/canceled orders have an
   estimated delivery date and 542 of 542 of their item rows have a shipping
   limit date, while delivered-carrier and delivered-customer stamps are 0 on
   every one of those statuses except the 6 canceled rows that carry a delivery
   stamp (source data noise; recorded rather than smoothed over).
2. Fill rate by itself hides the survivorship trap the brief points at: 96,478
   of 99,441 orders are delivered (97.02 %), so any column filled at delivery
   looks 97 % present. The audit therefore treats 'present for the statuses that
   never completed' as the test, not 'present overall'.
